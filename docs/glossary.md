# Glossary

**Angle cock.** Valve at each end of a car's brake pipe. Closed, it isolates the pipe behind it.
**Ballast.** Crushed rock the ties sit in. Drains, spreads load, holds alignment.
**Bad order.** A car flagged defective, to be set out and routed to a shop.
**Bottle the air.** Close angle cocks before uncoupling so the standing cut keeps its pipe pressure and does not dump into emergency.
**Bowl.** The fan of classification tracks below a hump.
**Buff.** Compressive force through the couplers. Opposite of draft.
**Cant.** Superelevation: raising the outer rail on a curve.
**Class (track).** Speed rating by condition. FRA classes 1 through 5 and up.
**Consist.** The ordered list of locomotives and cars making up a train.
**Cut.** A group of cars handled together. Also an excavation through high ground.
**CWR.** Continuous welded rail. No joints, smooth ride, needs a welding plant.
**Davis equation.** Empirical rolling resistance model for rail vehicles.
**Dead on the law.** A crew that has reached its hours-of-service limit and must stop.
**Doubling.** Splitting a long train across two yard tracks and reassembling it later.
**DPU.** Distributed power: remote-controlled locomotives mid-train or at the rear.
**Draft.** Tension through the couplers.
**Draft gear.** Spring-damper behind each coupler that absorbs impacts.
**ECP.** Electronically controlled pneumatic brakes. All cars apply at once.
**Fill.** Embankment built from excavated or borrowed material.
**Flood loader.** Silo over a track that loads passing cars without stopping the train.
**Frog.** The X-shaped casting where two rails cross in a turnout. Its number sets the diverging angle and speed.
**Grade.** Slope as a ratio or percent. Ruling grade is the steepest sustained grade that sets a line's tonnage rating.
**Helper.** Extra locomotive added for a grade and removed after.
**Hump.** An artificial hill cars roll down by gravity to be sorted.
**Hunting.** Self-excited yaw oscillation of a truck above a critical speed.
**Indexer.** Arm at a rotary dumper that advances the train one car at a time.
**Interchange.** Where cars pass between railroads. Here, the map edge.
**Kick.** Accelerate a car and uncouple it so it rolls into a track on its own.
**Knuckle.** The hinged jaw of a coupler. The part that breaks under excess draft.
**L/V ratio.** Lateral over vertical wheel force. High values climb the rail.
**Manifest.** A train of mixed cars with individual destinations.
**Mass-haul diagram.** Cumulative cut minus fill along an alignment, used to plan earth movement.
**MGT.** Million gross tons. Track wear counter.
**Muskeg.** Peat bog. Terrible ground for embankments.
**Retarder.** Rail-mounted clamp in a hump yard that slows rolling cars.
**Rock-and-roll.** Resonant roll on staggered jointed rail at a specific speed.
**Rotary coupler.** Coupler that lets a car rotate for dumping while still coupled.
**Run-in, run-out.** Slack bunching or stretching along a train.
**Saw-by.** Maneuver to pass two trains where neither fits the siding.
**Set out.** Leave a car on a siding, usually because it is bad order.
**Shove to a joint.** Push a cut until it couples.
**Slack.** Free play in couplers. Sums to meters over a long train.
**Split switch.** Derailment from entering a turnout set against the move at its points.
**Spoil.** Excavated material with no use on site.
**Stringlining.** Cars pulled toward the inside of a curve by draft force until they derail. Empties are prone.
**Sub-ballast.** Gravel layer under the ballast over the subgrade.
**Tamping.** Packing ballast under ties to restore geometry.
**Trim.** Assembling outbound trains at the bottom of a hump bowl.
**Truck.** Bogie: the wheel assembly under each end of a car.
**Turnout.** A switch plus its frog and closure rails. The whole diverging assembly.
**Unit train.** One commodity, one origin, one destination, cycling.
**Waybill.** The routing and billing record for one car's load.
**Wayside detector.** Trackside sensor for hot bearings or dragging equipment.
