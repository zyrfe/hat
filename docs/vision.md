# Vision

## Pitch

An overhead view of a rail network you build and run, where the trains are stupidly long
and the game never lies about it. A mile of freight has slack that runs in and out, brakes
that reach the rear ten seconds late, and a length that has to fit somewhere at every yard,
siding and terminal it touches. You lay the track, move the ballast, hire the crews, and
live with the grades you chose.

## Pillars

1. **Length is felt.** Every system notices train length: coupler slack, brake propagation,
   siding fit, yard track fit, terminal dwell, crew hours, brake test time.
2. **No cheating.** There is no infinite depot and no magic station. A train is always
   somewhere on the network. Terminals are small structures the train moves through, the
   way the real bulk industry does it.
3. **Everything is a thing.** Rail, ties, ballast, spoil, fuel, machines, crews. Each exists
   in the world, is moved by the network or by road, and costs time, money and labor.
   Lines do not poof into existence.
4. **Engineering, not optimization.** Every construction and operating choice is a trade-off
   with no dominant answer: grade vs. distance vs. tunnel, jointed vs. welded rail, single
   track with sidings vs. double track, helpers vs. distributed power, timber trestle now
   vs. steel later.
5. **Convincing physics, cheap math.** Trains are a 1D problem along the track with faux
   lateral motion layered on. Rigid bodies appear only when something goes wrong.
6. **SI internally.** Meters, kilograms, seconds, newtons, pascals. Display units are a
   player setting. No barleycorn-grains of torque.

## What it is not

- Not a cab simulator. The camera is overhead, tiltable, zoomable.
- Not a rigid-body sim. Cars are a box model on a 1D path until they derail.
- Not a tycoon with abstract stations and instant construction.
- Not a puzzle with a correct answer. Different railroads should be viable.

## The fantasy

A 200-car unit train creeping out of a loop-track flood loader at walking pace while the
hump yard across the valley sorts a manifest one car at a time, and knowing that every
meter of that track was cut, filled, ballasted and laid by machines you scheduled around
revenue trains.

## Genre notes

| Game | Take from it |
|---|---|
| Factorio | Rail signal blocks, path reservation, building with what you produce |
| OpenTTD | Network scale, schedule UI, long-term map ownership |
| NIMBY Rails | Real-scale 1D train sim, immediate-mode tool UI, thousands of trains |
| Rail Route | Dispatcher UI, interlocking as gameplay |
| Transport Fever 2 | Terrain-following track, grades and cut/fill as visible cost |
| Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic | Every ton and every worker is delivered; construction is logistics |
| Railroader | Yard work feel: hand brakes, air, kicking cars, coupling by feel |
| Derail Valley | Coupling, brake pipe and slack presented viscerally |
| Transport Tycoon Deluxe | The vehicle window: a live viewport that follows one vehicle with its controls beside it |
| Star Control II | Captain portraits and a status panel that make a machine feel crewed |
| Kerbal Space Program | Crew portraits that react to what you just did |
